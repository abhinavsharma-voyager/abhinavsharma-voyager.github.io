  1. 2023

    Graduate Project

    Vehicle Control in the CARLA Simulator: PID and Predictive Steering

    North Carolina State University · CSC 591 Embedded/Real-Time Systems for Autonomous Driving with Machine Learning

    • Problem: drive an automated vehicle along a route in the CARLA simulator as quickly as possible while minimizing lane invasions and collisions.
    • Approach: built a custom CARLA agent with tuned longitudinal (speed) and lateral (steering) PID controllers, and added collision and lane-invasion counters to measure performance. Then replaced the PID steering with a sampling-based predictive controller that simulates candidate steering angles over a short horizon with a kinematic vehicle model, selects the angle that best tracks upcoming waypoints, and limits steering at higher speeds.
    • Result: the tuned PID agent completed the route in 21.6 s at speeds above 70 km/h, with 5 lane invasions.

    • PID Control
    • Predictive Control
    • Python

  4. 2022

    Graduate Project

    Driving an Underpowered Car Up a Mountain

    University of Massachusetts Lowell · COMP 5435 Reinforcement Learning

    • Problem: the car is underpowered and must first move away from the goal to build momentum. The state (position and velocity) is continuous, so tabular methods cannot be used.
    • Approach: approximated the action-value function with Fourier-basis features and learned with episodic semi-gradient SARSA, including n-step variants (n = 1, 8, 16). Also applied actor-critic with eligibility traces, a policy-gradient method, to the same task.
    • Result: the best n depended on the step size: with a low step size, n = 16 reached the goal in fewer steps early on; with a high step size, n = 1 did better. Given enough episodes, the variants performed similarly.
    • Function Approximation
    • Fourier Basis
    • Semi-Gradient SARSA
    • Actor-Critic
    • Continuous State Space

  5. 2022

    Graduate Project

    N-Step SARSA Across Four Environments

    University of Massachusetts Lowell · COMP 5435 Reinforcement Learning

    • Problem: evaluate how n-step SARSA performs in environments with different reward structures: terminal-only reward (Blackjack, Frozen Lake) and per-step reward (Taxi, Cliff Walking), including one stochastic environment (Frozen Lake).
    • Approach: implemented n-step SARSA and ran hyperparameter sensitivity analyses for each environment. Used n = ∞ (Monte Carlo) where reward arrives only at the end of an episode and n = 1 where reward arrives at every step.
    • Result: the agent learned to complete the Taxi task, with ε = 0.1 and a decaying ε giving good returns. The Frozen Lake policy was checked under slippery conditions and produced an appropriate path.

  6. 2022

    Graduate Project

    Comparing Temporal-Difference Control Algorithms: SARSA, Q-Learning, and Double Q-Learning

    University of Massachusetts Lowell · COMP 5435 Reinforcement Learning

    • Problem: compare temporal-difference control methods on Cliff Walking, where stepping off the cliff gives a reward of −100, and on a gridworld with stochastic rewards.
    • Approach: implemented SARSA, Q-learning, and Double Q-learning with ε-greedy exploration (fixed and decaying ε). Cliff Walking used 50 independent runs of 500 episodes each (ε = 0.1, α = 0.5, undiscounted).
    • Result: with a fixed ε, SARSA learned the safer path; with a decaying ε, it also moved toward the optimal, riskier path. On the stochastic-reward gridworld, Double Q-learning found a better policy than Q-learning, consistent with it avoiding maximization bias.
    • SARSA
    • Q-Learning
    • Double Q-Learning
    • On-Policy vs. Off-Policy
    • Python

  1. 2024

    Graduate Project

    Performance Evaluation of a Freeway Segment Using TransModeler 7.0

    North Carolina State University · CE 507 Sensors, Instrumentation, and Data Analytics for Transportation Networks

    • Problem: where and how does congestion form on a 5-mile, 5-interchange segment of the I-264 freeway in Portsmouth, Virginia, during the morning peak?
    • Approach: used TransModeler microsimulation to analyze traffic demand, headways, speeds, travel times, and lane-changing behavior across segments and over time.
    • Result: merge and diverge zones were the main bottlenecks; congestion built rapidly between 6:30 and 8:00 AM, with downstream speeds falling to 10–30 mph and travel times rising from 50–60 s to 70–90 s, growing at about 0.055 min/mile per minute.
    • TransModeler
    • Traffic Microsimulation
    • Freeway Operations
    • Bottleneck Analysis
    • Congestion Analysis

    Graduate Project

    Effect of Autonomous Vehicles on Intersection Capacity

    North Carolina State University · CE 502 Traffic Operations

    • Problem: how will automated vehicles change the capacity of signalized intersections, when standard HCM methods assume human-driven traffic?
    • Approach: wrote a review paper grouping the literature into capacity estimation methods, capacity improvement strategies, and capacity analysis, and proposed a field test-bed for studying AVs at a signalized intersection.
    • Result: most studies report capacity gains (around 40% at full AV penetration), but the evidence comes mainly from simulation with unverified AV parameters, and some studies report decreases, highlighting the need for field studies.

  3. 2023

    Graduate Project

    Validating Control Delay Estimates at a Signalized Intersection

    North Carolina State University · CE 502 Traffic Operations

    • Problem: does the HCM control delay model match the delay measured in the field at a real signalized intersection (Avent Ferry Rd and Varsity Dr, Raleigh, NC)?
    • Approach: in a team of five, video-recorded the through lane during the evening peak, used regression on cumulative arrivals and departures to estimate arrival rate and saturation flow, and computed capacity and HCM control delay.
    • Result: the modeled delay of 32.7 s/veh closely matched the 29.6 s/veh measured in the field (v/c = 0.69, LOS C).

    Graduate Project

    Capabilities and Limitations of Car-Following Models: Newell, GM, IDM, and Gipps

    University of Massachusetts Lowell · CIVE 5490 Traffic Flow Theory

    • Problem: how realistic are standard car-following models, and how well do they reproduce real automated vehicle (ACC) following?
    • Approach: simulated 20 vehicles behind an oscillating leader using the Newell, GM, IDM, and Gipps models, then compared IDM and Gipps predictions with three real ACC follower trajectories.
    • Result: IDM and Gipps damped speed oscillations in simulation, but the real ACC followers still showed oscillations and speed differences that the models did not capture.
